What it checks

CheckWhy it bites
Coordinate order[lat, lon] instead of GeoJSON's [lon, lat] silently relocates a Ghanaian cocoa plot to the Atlantic. The file parses. Nothing complains.
Ring closureA polygon whose first and last position differ is not a polygon. Many export tools omit the closing point.
Self-intersectionA bowtie ring has no well-defined interior, so its area is meaningless and its containment tests are wrong.
The 4-hectare rulePlots above 4 ha need a polygon; a point is not sufficient. Declaring 10 ha with a single coordinate is a rejected statement.
Declared vs computed areaIf your stated hectares and your geometry disagree by more than 2×, one of them is wrong — usually a unit or a coordinate-order error.
HS ↔ commodity consistencyAn HS code that maps to cocoa alongside a declared commodity of coffee is an internal contradiction on the face of the statement.
Cut-off assertionA DDS must positively assert the commodity was not produced on land deforested after 31 December 2020. Silence is not compliance.
What this does not do. It does not check deforestation — not against satellite imagery, not against any forest baseline. It does not verify legality of production in the country of origin. It does not file anything with the EU Information System. It tells you whether your statement is well-formed, which is a necessary condition and nowhere near a sufficient one.
